# 30-Day Discovery and Outreach Plan

## Goal

Find one narrow customer problem that merits a paid, bounded visual-positioning evaluation. This is a discovery plan, not evidence that outreach or customer validation has already happened.

## Initial segment hypothesis

Start with **museums, galleries, showrooms, and visitor-facing venues** that have one specific indoor or outdoor zone where visitors need a location-aware AR or navigation experience. AR agencies with a named venue customer are a secondary segment. Do not pursue hospitals or safety-critical workflows first unless a specific buyer requests a non-critical, tightly bounded evaluation.

The segment is a hypothesis. Revisit it based on buyer access, urgency, site/data access, and willingness to fund a pilot—not on the number of portal features a competitor advertises.

## 30-day targets

These are activity goals, not completed results:

- Research 30 named organizations and identify a plausible buyer/contact for each.
- Send 20 individually tailored first-contact messages, after reviewing the target and message.
- Complete 6 qualified discovery conversations.
- Produce up to 2 scoped pilot briefs for qualified prospects; send a proposal only when the buyer confirms a problem, access path, decision process, and budget range.
- Record a segment decision at day 30: continue, adjust, or stop, with evidence from conversations.

Do not claim a pilot is validated, a price is accepted, or a customer is committed unless that outcome is documented by the customer.

## Weekly cadence

| Days | Focus | Work | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | Focus and prepare | Select one primary use case; review the pilot one-pager; create a 30-account list; identify buyer roles and a relevant venue signal for each | Named list with source, hypothesis, and contact route |
| 6–12 | First outreach | Send 10 tailored messages; follow up once after 4–5 business days; book discovery calls | Message date, channel, reply, and next step recorded per account |
| 13–19 | Learn and qualify | Send 10 more tailored messages; hold discovery calls; ask about pain, smallest useful area, access, success criteria, budget, procurement, and decision timing | Call notes and qualification status; no unsupported product claims |
| 20–26 | Scope real opportunities | For qualified prospects, confirm data path and deployment constraints with engineering; draft a pilot brief with customer-specific targets and responsibilities | Prospect-reviewed scope or explicit blocker; no assumed input support |
| 27–30 | Decide | Review conversion from account to reply, call, qualified opportunity, and scope; summarize repeated objections and select next segment/action | Dated day-30 decision memo and next-month targets |

## Qualification gate

Advance an account to pilot scoping only when there is evidence for each item:

1. A specific problem and affected users.
2. A bounded area and a plausible demo scenario.
3. A buyer or sponsor and a decision process.
4. Site/data access and permission to capture or evaluate.
5. A technical contact able to confirm devices, network, storage, and input format.
6. A budget path and timing compatible with a paid, time-bounded engagement.
7. Customer-agreed acceptance measures that can be evaluated with available ground truth.

If a required input or deployment path is not confirmed, record it as a blocker and do not promise compatibility. Do not position Google 3D as an authorized commercial training or deployment source; any such use requires separately verified permission.

## Discovery call outline

- What user or operational problem happens in this space today?
- Which smallest route/zone would make a successful evaluation meaningful?
- How do you currently handle indoor location or persistent AR placement?
- What result would justify continuing, and how can it be measured?
- Can the team provide site access, capture permission, representative devices, and ground truth?
- Who owns the budget and who approves data/security terms?
- What is the decision date and what procurement steps apply?
- What would make this evaluation a clear no-go?

## Message principles

- Describe the offer as a supported evaluation of visual positioning and anchors in one bounded area.
- State that performance is measured on agreed real-device samples and may fail to meet the target.
- Do not claim centimeter accuracy, broad scanner compatibility, tenant isolation, production SLA, or competitor-feature parity without current evidence and a specific approved scope.
- Ask for a short discovery call; do not send customer data or ask for scans before data-handling and access terms are agreed.

## Tracking fields

Keep one row per account in a private working tracker:

| Field | Example / guidance |
|---|---|
| Organization and segment | Named venue; museum, gallery, showroom, agency |
| Source and reason for fit | Publicly observed venue/use-case signal |
| Buyer/contact and contact route | Name/role or role to identify; do not guess personal contact details |
| Outreach date/channel/message | Record each touch and follow-up |
| Response and call date | Include explicit no-response state |
| Pain/use case | Customer's own description |
| Area/data/access readiness | Confirmed, unknown, or blocked |
| Budget/decision process/timing | Customer-stated or unknown |
| Qualification | Researching, contacted, discovery, qualified, scoped, proposal, won/lost |
| Next step, owner, due date | One concrete action |
| Evidence/notes | Link call notes or customer-approved materials |
